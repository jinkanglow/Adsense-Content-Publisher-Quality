# Developmental editing — source-grounded procedure
Source: Scott Norton, *Developmental Editing: A Handbook for Freelancers, Authors, and Publishers*, **2009 edition as verified from the uploaded PDF copyright page** (filename incorrectly describes 2023 second edition). Introduction and chapters 1–10.

## Macro before micro
1. Concept: identify audience, reader problem, editorial promise and boundaries (ch. 1).
2. Content: map what exists, what's missing and the writer's actual source material (ch. 2).
3. Thesis: distinguish subject/topic from falsifiable, defensible central claim; avoid rehashing (ch. 3).
4. Narrative: decide when chronology is needed, distinguish timelines from arguments (ch. 4).
5. Exposition: create claim -> evidence -> mechanism -> competing explanation -> implication sequence (ch. 5).
6. Plan: issue a section-by-section developmental blueprint before large-scale rewrite (ch. 6).
7. Rhythm: remove redundant passages, balance sections by argumentative weight, not equal word count (ch. 7).
8. Transitions: bridge missing logical steps; place conclusions where evidence earns them (ch. 8).
9. Style: preserve author's distinct voice and justified confidence; correct overstatement without generic hedging (ch. 9).
10. Display: use headings, tables and graphics only when they improve comprehension (ch. 10).

## Execution modes
- LINE_REPAIR: facts, wording, transitions, no change in thesis.
- DEVELOPMENTAL_REWRITE: restructure with preservation ledger (thesis, unique insights, supported figures, counterarguments, audience, voice).
- RESEARCH_REQUIRED: cannot responsibly fill a missing empirical premise; deliver a conditional draft and evidence request rather than inventing reporting.

## Change accounting
Record removed claims, relocated arguments, retained figures, added sourced material, unresolved dependencies, and editorial rationale. Never transform a critical or opinion piece into promotional copy merely for perceived AdSense eligibility. An article can be compliant and critical.
